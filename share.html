What this is

Clearview Golf Club turns 80 this year. The National Black Golf Hall of Fame turns 40. Together that is 120 years, and the two are marking it on one weekend in Canton.

The short version

Celebration 120: 80 years of Clearview Golf Club and 40 years of the National Black Golf Hall of Fame.

Friday and Saturday, October 2 and 3, 2026, in East Canton and Canton, Ohio.

In 1946 William Powell, a World War II Army veteran, came home to find himself unwelcome on public courses. He built Clearview Golf Club by hand out of a dairy farm. His family has run "America's Course" ever since. In 1986 Harold Dunovant, the first Black graduate of the PGA Business School, founded the National Black Golf Hall of Fame.

The Weekend Pass is $600 and covers all of it: Friday evening at Clearview, a swag bag, Saturday golf at "America's Course", and the Closing Reception and Dinner at the Pro Football Hall of Fame. Four passes are $2,000, which is $500 each. A dinner seat on its own is $150.

One paragraph, for an email or a newsletter

On October 2 and 3, Clearview Golf Club in East Canton, Ohio marks 80 years since William Powell built it by hand, and the National Black Golf Hall of Fame marks 40 years since Harold Dunovant founded it. The two have joined together for one weekend: an evening with Clearview and the Hall on Friday, golf at "America's Course" on Saturday, and a Closing Reception and Dinner at the Pro Football Hall of Fame in Canton on Saturday evening. A Weekend Pass covering all three is $600, a foursome is $2,000, and a dinner seat alone is $150. Registration and the silent auction are at blackgolfhof.org/register and auction.in4pargolf.com.
